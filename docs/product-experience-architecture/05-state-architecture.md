# State Architecture

The Eli product should regulate the experience through `Shelter`, `Focus`, and `Momentum`.

These are not different products and not separate visual themes. They are three intensities of the same product, tuned to the student's current capacity.

## What State Changes

At the product level, state should change:

- how much is visible
- how many decisions are exposed
- how direct the product sounds
- how strong the hierarchy feels
- how assertive the next action feels

State should **not** change:

- Eli's identity
- the product's main loop
- the core ethical posture of the experience

## Shelter

`Shelter` should be used when the student's current capacity is low and the product needs to reduce threat before asking for effort.

### Product behavior in Shelter
- the number of visible choices should be lowest
- one next step should dominate the surface
- copy should be shortest and most grounding
- hierarchy should be soft and easy to parse
- actions should feel reversible and low-commitment
- secondary information should recede aggressively

### What Shelter is protecting against
- overwhelm
- shame
- avoidance triggered by complexity
- decision fatigue

## Focus

`Focus` is the default state of the product when the student can hold a normal amount of structure.

### Product behavior in Focus
- the surface should present enough context to orient the student
- hierarchy should be clear and practical
- copy should be direct without becoming managerial
- actions should feel bounded and useful
- support information may remain visible if it helps continuity

### What Focus is protecting against
- ambiguity
- scattered attention
- unnecessary detours

## Momentum

`Momentum` should be used when the student can tolerate more decisiveness and a stronger sense of forward motion.

### Product behavior in Momentum
- the product may show slightly richer support structure
- hierarchy may become crisper
- CTAs may feel firmer, but never aggressive
- copy may be more decisive and forward-moving
- the experience may reduce hedging and over-cushioning

### What Momentum is protecting against
- hesitation
- stalled continuation
- under-directed energy

## State Variables

The main product variables influenced by state are:

| Variable | Shelter | Focus | Momentum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choice count | Lowest | Moderate | Moderate-plus |
| Copy posture | Grounding | Practical | More decisive |
| Hierarchy | Soft | Clear | Crisp |
| CTA energy | Reversible | Bounded | Stronger but calm |
| Visible context | Minimal | Useful | Richer support |
| Reflection allowance | Rare | Limited | Higher if phase allows |

## Product Rule

State should always be used to make the product more fitting, not to make it feel more personalized for its own sake.

If state changes become too visible or too theatrical, the regulation has become performance.
