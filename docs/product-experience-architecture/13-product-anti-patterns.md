# Product Anti-Patterns

This document defines what Eli must never become as a product.

The fastest way to weaken Eli is not usually through obvious mistakes. It is through plausible product moves that quietly pull the experience toward pressure, performance, or feature sprawl.

## 1. Performance Dashboard

Eli must not become a product where the first job is reviewing performance.

Avoid:

- analytics-first entry points
- overview-heavy home surfaces
- pressure-heavy progress summaries
- self-review before re-entry

## 2. Gamified Habit Machine

Eli must not rely on streaks, badges, points, or completion spectacle to create motivation.

These patterns replace real progress with extrinsic pressure and damage the trust posture of the product.

## 3. Cheerleading Productivity App

Eli must not sound or behave like a hype-driven productivity coach.

Avoid:

- inflated positivity
- urgency theater
- performance slogans
- motivational pressure standing in for product clarity

## 4. Over-Personalized AI Companion Theater

Eli must not pretend to know the student more deeply than it has earned.

Avoid:

- premature intimacy
- speculative pattern claims
- emotionally manipulative personalization
- reflection that sounds diagnostic or scripted

## 5. Feature-Sprawl Study Suite

Eli must not become a broad software ecosystem for every study need.

Avoid:

- too many top-level destinations
- too many utilities competing for attention
- management surfaces that outweigh actual study support
- complexity added in the name of comprehensiveness

## 6. Decorative Brand Product

Eli must not rely on layered-on brand styling to create identity.

Avoid:

- logo-heavy flows
- branded chrome
- decorative illustrations inside core study moments
- visual noise added to make the product feel "special"

## 7. Reflection Inflation

Eli must not overuse `Mirror`.

If reflection appears too often, too early, or too confidently, it stops feeling meaningful and starts feeling like brand performance.

## Final Rule

If a product decision makes Eli feel:

- more managerial
- more performative
- more judgmental
- more decorative
- more feature-heavy

then the decision is probably pulling Eli away from its core product architecture.
