# Interview Script: Faculty Teachers

## Goal of this Interview

To understand the teacher's perspective on "Silent Decay," validate their role as a key influencer, and test their reaction to a tool that could empower their mentorship.

---

### **1. Introduction & Framing (5 mins)**

- "Hi [Name], thank you for your time. I'm [Your Name], and I'm a product strategist exploring ways to better support both students and teachers in the competitive exam journey."
- "I'm particularly interested in your experience on the front lines, as you see things that directors and even parents might miss. My goal is simply to learn from your expertise."
- "To start, what do you find is the most rewarding part of teaching and mentoring your students?"

---

### **2. Problem Discovery: The Teacher's View (15 mins)**

_Our goal is to understand their world and their pains._

- "Beyond the raw test scores, what are the subtle signs or behaviors you look for to know if a student is genuinely on track versus just going through the motions?"
- "Can you tell me about a time you had a gut feeling a student was starting to struggle, even though their grades hadn't dropped yet? What were the signs you noticed?"
- "How much of your time is spent on proactive, one-on-one mentoring versus classroom teaching or reactive problem-solving?"
- "What is the most frustrating or challenging part of trying to support students who might be silently struggling with burnout, anxiety, or bad study habits?"

---

### **3. Solution Exploration: A "Mentoring Superpower" (10 mins)**

_We frame the solution as a tool for them, to empower them._

- "We're exploring an idea to help with exactly that. Imagine you had a private, simple dashboard."
- "This dashboard would give you a 'heads-up' on which of your students are showing early behavioral signs of burnout, avoidance, or inefficient learning—all based on data from a private app the student uses."
- "It wouldn't be another complex system to manage. Think of it more like a 'check engine light' for your students, allowing you to know exactly who to have a quick, supportive conversation with."
- "What is your initial gut reaction to a tool like that?"
- "How would having this kind of insight change how you interact with your students?"
- "What would be your biggest concern about a tool like this? (e.g., 'I don't have time for another dashboard', 'What if the data is wrong?', 'Will students feel spied on?')."

---

### **4. Wrap-up (5 mins)**

- "This feedback is incredibly valuable. Your perspective is crucial because, for any tool like this to work, teachers have to be at the center of it."
- "Is there anything else you think I should know about the challenges teachers face in this environment?"
- "Thank you again for your expert insights."
