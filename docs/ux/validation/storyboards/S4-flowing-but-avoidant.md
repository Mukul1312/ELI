# S4 Storyboard — The Flowing-but-Avoidant Student

> 20 sessions across 12 calendar days. Every session shows full Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 ASCII screens. Session-based numbering with calendar annotations. All systems applied: pattern claim tiers, task types, topic recommendation, silence/re-entry, formation period rules.

## Archetype profile

From [11-archetype-experience-map.md](../11-archetype-experience-map.md):


| Trait                     | Value                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| **Primary mechanic**      | "It Knows Me" (mirror)                                                                                |
| **Phase 1 mode**          | Neutral → Rescue at P3                                                                                |
| **Phase 3 key scenario**  | Deal card at SessionEnd (verification push)                                                           |
| **Escalation inflection** | Day 4–5 (honest mirror about avoidance)                                                               |

| **Copy rule**             | Frame verification as closure, not testing. "Prove it to yourself" not "Let's test what you learned." |
| **Identity statement**    | "Your sessions count now."                                                                            |

## Design Lessons from S4

### 1. The intervention is at Phase 3, not Phase 1

S4's Phase 1 is fine. They open the app, they study. The problem surfaces only at SessionEnd when the verification question appears. This is why the deal card mechanic exists: the ask happens at the moment of maximum receptivity (just finished studying), not at the moment of maximum resistance (opening the app).

### 2. The shrunk deal card works

Removing "Don't want to answer now" after 3 consecutive defers forces a binary answer. This sounds coercive but isn't — the question is simpler ("Just a tap"), the options are clearer (Yes/Not yet), and neither answer is punished. "Not yet" keeps the loop open without shame. The escape hatch was enabling avoidance, not protecting the student.

### 3. "Not yet" is not failure

Session 5 (Act 1) and Session 15 (Act 3) both produced "Not yet." But they mean different things. Session 5's "Not yet" led to topic avoidance (Session 6 was a new topic). Session 15's "Not yet" led to a targeted retry (Session 16 closed it). The system treats both the same — revisit stays open. The student's relationship to the question changed, not the system's response.

### 4. The honest mirror unlocks the pivot

Session 10's greeting — "9 sessions. 5 revisits open. None closed yet." — is the turning point. It's Tier 3 language (factual, confident), not judgment. The student reads it and knows it's true. ELI didn't say "you should close loops" or "your study isn't effective." It said what it observed. The student drew their own conclusion.

### 5. Verification sessions are short

Once the student starts closing loops, Revise sessions are ~10-15 minutes. They're not re-studying — they're checking recall. This means the "cost" of verification is low: a 10-minute session that drops the counter by 1. The low cost is why the loop becomes self-sustaining — the reward (counter dropping) is high relative to the effort (10-minute check).

### 6. Identity moment timing matters

"Your sessions count now" at Session 17 works because it's true at Session 17 and would NOT have been true at Session 7. At Session 7, the student's sessions didn't count — they were studying without proving retention. The eligibility gates (vRate7d ≥ 0.70, pendingChecks ≤ 2) ensure the statement is earned, not aspirational.
