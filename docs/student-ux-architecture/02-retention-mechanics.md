# Retention Mechanics — What Creates Pull

> Five named mechanics that translate the craving loop beats into concrete product behavior. Each mechanic has a specific activation window, psychological foundation, and strict design rules. If a mechanic is implemented without its rules, it will backfire.

---

## How to read this document

Each mechanic follows the same structure:

1. **What it is** — one paragraph definition
2. **Where in storyboards** — specific scene + day citations from S2, S5, S7
3. **Psychological principle** — the research-backed reason it works
4. **Craving loop beat** — which beat from [01-craving-loop.md](01-craving-loop.md) this mechanic powers
5. **Design rules** — do's and don'ts that protect the mechanic from misuse

## Mechanic 1: "It Knows Me" (Personalization Signals)

**Activation window:** Session 14+ (after minimum 7 sessions with sufficient data; Tier 3+ pattern claims required — see [16-formation-period.md](16-formation-period.md))

The app reflects the student's own behavioral pattern back to them in a single, specific sentence. This is not a recommendation engine. It is a mirror: "here is what I noticed about you." The student feels seen — not diagnosed, not scored, not categorized — just seen. The power comes from the specificity. A generic "you're doing great" creates nothing. A specific "when you pick Sharp (5), you do best with 1 pending check first, then hard work" creates ownership.

**Where in storyboards:**

- **S7 Scene 9 (Day 14):** *"When you pick Sharp (5), you do best with 1 pending check first, then hard work."* — ELI reflects S7's pattern of rushing into new topics before clearing debt
- **S5 Scene 9 (Day 14):** *"When you pick Okay (3), a short block + 1 check works best."* — ELI reflects S5's optimal session shape after the re-entry arc
- Both hints appear as small text below the scale input, not as a card or modal

**Psychological principle:** The IKEA effect applied to coaching. When people invest effort into building something, they value it disproportionately. When the system reflects YOUR pattern — a pattern you created through your own sessions — you feel ownership of the coaching relationship. The app becomes "my app" rather than "an app."

**Craving loop beat:** **Recognition** — this mechanic deepens the Recognition beat from generic state-awareness (Day 1-7) to personal pattern reflection (Day 14+). See [01-craving-loop.md](01-craving-loop.md) Beat 2.

**Design rules:**

- Never activate before 7 completed sessions — the system has not earned the right to reflect before that
- Never frame as diagnosis ("You are a morning studier") — always frame as observation ("When you pick X, Y works best")
- One hint per screen, never two — multiple hints feel like surveillance, not recognition
- Keep it to one sentence — brevity signals confidence; long explanations signal insecurity
- Place below the scale input as small text — it should feel like a whisper, not an announcement

## Mechanic 2: Loop Closure Satisfaction (The Counter Drop)

**Activation window:** Session 4+ (when pending checks begin accumulating)

The core dopamine mechanic. The number going down. `Pending checks: 3 → 2`. Not points. Not streaks. Not badges. Not XP. A real number that represents a real thing — topics the student studied but hasn't proven they remember. When the number drops, something was open and now it's closed. That is the reward. The entire product is architecturally designed to make this moment happen once per session.

**Where in storyboards:**

- **S7's entire 21-day arc** is structured around this mechanic. S7 starts by collecting new topics (pending checks grow), hits a turning point, then shifts to closing them (pending checks shrink)
- **S7 Scene 5 (Day 5):** *"Pending checks stops growing and starts shrinking"* — the pivot moment where S7 shifts from accumulation to closure
- **S7 Scene 12 (Day 21):** `Pending checks: 1–2 (bounded)` — the consistent outcome of loop closure working over 21 days
- **All three storyboards, Scene 12 (Day 21):** pending checks are bounded at 1-2, proving the mechanic worked across archetypes

**Psychological principle:** The Zeigarnik effect. Uncompleted tasks occupy more mental space than completed ones, creating persistent cognitive tension. Closing a pending check provides disproportionate psychological relief — far more than the effort would suggest. The counter going from 3 to 2 feels better than studying for an hour with no counter change. The mechanic converts study effort into visible, countable closure.

**Craving loop beat:** **Closure** — this mechanic IS the Closure beat. The `3 → 2` animation is the single most important visual in Phase 3. See [01-craving-loop.md](01-craving-loop.md) Beat 4.

**Design rules:**

- The counter change (`3 → 2`) must be visually prominent — animated, not buried in copy or footnotes
- When `pending checks = 0`, this is a milestone: special copy, distinct card, celebration-appropriate moment
- Never show the counter going UP in Phase 3 — if new pending checks are created by deferral, surface them in Phase 1 of the next session, never in the current close
- The "loop closed" confirmation must feel complete — no "but also..." or "and don't forget..." after the counter drops
- The counter is always an integer and always visible in Phase 1 when pending checks exist — the student should always know where they stand

## Mechanic 3: The Micro-Win Entry (Impossibly Small Starts)

**Activation window:** Every session, but most critical on Sessions 1–3 and after silence re-entry (see [19-silence-and-reentry.md](19-silence-and-reentry.md))

The primary CTA is always the smallest viable action. Not the most productive action. Not the most efficient action. The smallest one. Five minutes. Two minutes. One question. The commitment is so small that saying no feels harder than saying yes. This is not about productivity — it is about crossing the activation threshold. Once the student has started (truly started, not planned), momentum carries them. The 2-minute start becomes a 15-minute session. But the 15-minute session never happens if the CTA said "15 minutes."

**Where in storyboards:**

- **S5 Scene 3 (Day 4):** *"5-minute win (recommended)"* — one button, no options, no decision fatigue. S5 is returning from silence and the bar must be as low as physically possible
- **S2 Scene 2 (Day 1):** *"Start 2 minutes, then decide"* — two buttons. The parenthetical "then decide" is load-bearing copy: it removes commitment anxiety by making the action reversible
- **S7 Scene 5 (Day 5):** *"Before you close: 1 question. That's it."* — the tiniest possible proof, offered as the student is about to leave
- **S5 Scene 3 (Day 4):** the entire Rescue mode screen has ONE button — the student cannot make a wrong choice because there is only one choice

**Psychological principle:** Behavioral activation. The hardest part of any task is the first 30 seconds. Not the middle, not the end — the beginning. Every minute of estimated effort above the resistance threshold increases the probability of freeze-to-zero. Reduce the commitment below the resistance threshold and the student acts. The "then decide" pattern is especially powerful because it removes the perception of commitment entirely — "I'm not committing to studying, I'm just starting for 2 minutes and then I'll decide."

**Craving loop beat:** **Micro-win** — this mechanic IS the Micro-win beat. It produces the "I did something" feeling that feeds into Closure. See [01-craving-loop.md](01-craving-loop.md) Beat 3.

**Design rules:**

- The primary CTA must always be the smallest viable action — never the most productive one
- Never show an estimated time greater than 10 minutes on any primary CTA in Rescue mode
- "Then decide" is high-value copy — use it on pressure days and re-entry days to remove commitment anxiety
- The secondary CTA ("I can do more") is always visually secondary — never competing with the primary action
- After a micro-win is completed, the outcome must be visible: a loop closed, a counter changed, or a session recorded — effort without evidence creates doubt

## Mechanic 4: No Punishment = Trust = Return (Re-Entry Design)

**Activation window:** Every re-entry after silence (1+ day gap), but most powerful after 3+ day gaps (see [19-silence-and-reentry.md](19-silence-and-reentry.md) for silence duration tiers)

The app remembers the silence and says nothing about it. No "You've been away for 4 days." No "Welcome back! You missed 3 sessions." No gap counter. No streak-broken screen. No passive-aggressive "It's been a while..." The first words after silence lower the bar. They acknowledge the student is here — not where they were. This mechanic is invisible when it works. You only notice it by its absence: the things the app chose NOT to say.

**Where in storyboards:**

- **S5 Scene 3 (Day 4):** *"Welcome back. Today is not a comeback story. It's a 5-minute win."* — the app remembered the silence but said NOTHING about it. No duration reference. No "you missed 2 days." The very first sentence after silence lowers the bar
- **S5 Scene 1–3 (Day 1–4):** the entire S5 arc is a re-entry arc. Day 1 is a burnout crash. Day 2-3 are silence. Day 4 is re-entry. The app treats Day 4 as a new beginning, not a continuation of failure
- **S2 Scene 2 (Day 1):** *"Your drive is real. But today's pressure is high."* — even on Day 1, ELI's tone validates before redirecting. The pattern is the same: acknowledge, lower the bar, offer the smallest action

**Psychological principle:** Psychological safety. People return to environments that do not punish failure. Every "you missed X days" message, every streak-broken screen, every "it's been a while" passive-aggressive greeting trains the student to avoid the app after a gap. The cost of opening the app after silence must be zero. If the cost is shame, the student learns: "I can't open this app unless I've been consistent." That lesson is the death of retention for S5 (Burnout), S3 (Stalled Starter), and eventually everyone.

**Craving loop beat:** **Recognition** (re-entry variant) — this is a specialized form of the Recognition beat. Instead of "it noticed my pattern," it is "it noticed my absence and chose not to weaponize it." The trust this builds deepens all future Recognition beats. See [01-craving-loop.md](01-craving-loop.md) Beat 2.

**Design rules:**

- Never reference silence duration — not in copy, not in counters, not in analytics shown to the student
- Never show a gap counter or "days since last session" anywhere in the UI after a gap
- The first words after silence must lower the bar — the student must feel that today's ask is smaller, not bigger, because of the gap
- Never auto-expand the "Your Week" footer after silence — showing an empty week punishes the student with visual evidence of their gap
- The re-entry greeting must be warmer and shorter than a normal greeting — fewer words signal less judgment

## Mechanic 5: Identity Formation (Day 21 Mirror Statements)

**Activation window:** Session 20+ (or when behavioral threshold is crossed — not calendar-based). Eligibility: `verificationRate7d >= 0.70 AND pendingChecks <= 2 AND sessionsLast7d >= 4`. See [08-footer-and-identity.md](08-footer-and-identity.md) Part 2.

A single sentence that reframes what the student has been doing as who they are becoming. Not praise. Not a badge. Not a score. A mirror statement in second person, present tense, that names the identity shift the student's own behavior has created. "You're not just studying — you're closing the loop." This is the most powerful mechanic and the most dangerous to misuse. It only works if it is earned. Show it before the threshold and it feels hollow. Show it with the wrong framing and it feels manipulative.

**Where in storyboards:**

- **S7 Scene 12 (Day 21):** *"You're not just studying — you're closing the loop."* — names the shift from topic collection to loop closure that defines S7's entire arc
- **S5 Scene 12 (Day 21):** *"You came back."* — two words. The most powerful mirror statement in the system. After a burnout arc with silence and re-entry, the identity being named is resilience itself
- **S2 Scene 12 (Day 21):** *"Pressure days don't break you anymore — you still start."* — names the shift from freeze-guilt to consistent action under pressure
- Each statement is different because each archetype underwent a different transformation. The mirror reflects what actually happened, not a generic achievement.

**Psychological principle:** Identity-based habit formation (James Clear's framework). The most durable habits are those tied to identity ("I am someone who closes loops") rather than outcomes ("I study 4 hours a day"). Outcome-based habits collapse when outcomes fluctuate. Identity-based habits persist because the student is no longer doing a behavior — they are being a person. The mirror statement does not create the identity. The student's own behavior created it. The statement merely names what already exists.

**Craving loop beat:** A **6th beat** that emerges only at Day 21. This extends the 5-beat loop from [01-craving-loop.md](01-craving-loop.md) into a 6th beat: **Identity**. After the mirror statement, the loop no longer needs external hooks — the student's self-concept now includes "I am someone who does this." See the "How the Loop Compounds Over Time" table in 01-craving-loop.md, Day 21 row.

**Design rules:**

- The statement is earned, not scheduled — it appears only when a behavioral threshold is crossed (e.g., consistent loop closure for 14+ days, successful re-entry after silence, sustained action under pressure)
- Uses second person, present tense — "You are" / "You're" / "You came" — never past tense ("You used to...") which frames the old identity as the real one
- Never use "you used to..." language — it reminds the student of who they were, not who they are
- Immediately followed by a session start option — the mirror statement is not a destination; it is a doorway. No lingering, no celebration screen, no share button
- One statement per threshold crossing — never stack multiple identity statements. One mirror, one moment, then move on

## Mechanic Interaction Map

The five mechanics do not operate independently. Each reinforces at least one other mechanic, creating a web of mutual reinforcement.

| Mechanic | Reinforces | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Loop Closure** | **Anticipation** (Beat 1) | A shrinking counter creates curiosity about the next count — "will it go down again tomorrow?" |
| **Loop Closure** | **"It Knows Me"** | The closure pattern becomes the data that feeds personalization hints at Day 14+ |
| **"It Knows Me"** | **Micro-win** | A personalized hint makes the right starting action obvious — "when you pick Okay (3), a short block + 1 check works best" reduces decision fatigue |
| **"It Knows Me"** | **Identity Formation** | Seeing your own pattern reflected builds the self-concept that the Day 21 mirror statement names |
| **No Punishment** | **Recognition** (Beat 2) | Safe re-entry deepens trust, which makes all future Recognition beats land harder — the student believes the app is on their side |
| **No Punishment** | **Micro-win** | When the re-entry cost is zero, the student is willing to try the smallest action. Punishment raises the activation threshold; safety lowers it |
| **Micro-win** | **Loop Closure** | Starting is the prerequisite for closing. The micro-win gets the student into the session; loop closure rewards them for being there |
| **Micro-win** | **No Punishment** | Completing even a tiny action after silence proves that coming back was the right decision — it retroactively validates the safe re-entry |
| **Identity Formation** | **Anticipation** (Beat 1) | After the mirror statement, the student's self-concept includes "I am someone who does this" — anticipation becomes internal, no longer requiring external hooks |
| **Identity Formation** | **No Punishment** | A student with an identity-level habit is more likely to return after silence because the identity persists through gaps — "I'm still someone who closes loops, even if I missed a few days" |

## Summary: Mechanic Activation Timeline

> **Unit:** Session count, not calendar days. See [16-formation-period.md](16-formation-period.md) for the session-vs-day convention.

| Session range | Active mechanics | What the student experiences |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions 1–3 | Micro-win, No Punishment | Tiny starts, no judgment. The app earns the right to exist on their phone. |
| Sessions 4–7 | + Loop Closure | First `3 → 2` moment. The counter becomes meaningful. Closure feels real. |
| Sessions 8–13 | + (all four running) | Full mechanic set minus personalization. The routine forms. |
| Sessions 14–20 | + "It Knows Me" | Personalization hints activate. The app reflects the student's own patterns. |
| Sessions 20+ | + Identity Formation | Mirror statement earned. The loop becomes self-sustaining. |
