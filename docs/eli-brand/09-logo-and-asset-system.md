# Logo and Asset System

The Eli mark is a **signature, not a mascot**. Its job is to authenticate the space, signal presence, and give the brand a recognizable hand. It should never dominate the experience or behave like decoration layered on top of the product.

## Role of the Mark
The core Eli mark should feel:
- handwritten, but composed
- human, but not casual
- distinctive, but not attention-seeking
- quiet enough to live beside the product without becoming the product

The mark is not responsible for carrying the entire brand story. Its job is to signal identity and continuity while the rest of the brand is carried by tone, pace, spacing, and behavior.

## Brand Presence Rules

### 1. Signature, not chrome
The mark should support the experience rather than lead it.

- In product flows, the logo is secondary to the student's task and Eli's presence.
- In onboarding, landing, and launch surfaces, the logo can be more visible because orientation matters more there.
- The mark should become quieter as the student moves deeper into focused work.

### 2. Greeting before branding
**The greeting is the hero, not the brand chrome.**

- Logo presence should never overpower Eli's spoken presence.
- Brand recognition should come from consistent atmosphere and voice, not repetition of the mark.

### 3. No watermark behavior
- Do not place faded, decorative brand marks behind core product surfaces.
- Do not use the logo as ambient texture inside focused study flows.
- The product should feel like a clear, intentional space, not branded stationery.

## Mark System
The Eli mark system should support multiple levels of recognition without becoming fragmented.

### Primary form
- The full wordmark or full mark-plus-name form should be used when establishing identity matters.

### Secondary form
- A reduced mark can be used when recognition is already established or space is limited.

### Reduced forms
- Reduced forms must preserve recognizability, calmness, and distinctiveness.
- They must not collapse into generic notification-dot behavior or become visually ambiguous at small sizes.

## Reduced-Form Requirements
Small-scale brand artifacts such as favicons, app icons, and compact marks should satisfy these requirements:

- remain recognizable at very small sizes
- avoid visual ambiguity with generic UI indicators
- preserve enough character to feel like Eli
- work in both light and high-contrast contexts

The exact reduced-form solution should remain open until tested.

## Asset Expression Rules

### Illustration and icon posture
- Iconography should stay minimal and restrained.
- Utility icons should prioritize clarity over character.
- More expressive hand-drawn or sketch-like assets, if used, should be limited to marketing or orientation moments rather than core task flows.

### Contrast and reversal
- Reversed or contrast-adapted marks are valid when legibility requires them.
- The requirement is clarity and calm presence, not maximum visual drama.

## Design Constraint
Eli's mark should always feel like a signature of presence. If it starts behaving like a mascot, sticker, badge, or decorative stamp, it is being used too loudly.
