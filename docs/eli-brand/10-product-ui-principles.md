# Product UI Principles

These laws govern how the Eli brand translates into actual interface design. Every screen, component, and interaction must pass these tests.

## Law 1: One Calm Focal Point
Each screen should have one clear emotional and functional center:
- the welcome or orientation moment
- the current task or focal action
- the completion or reflection moment

Never compete with that center by adding extra branding, decorative assets, or secondary calls to action that are visually equally weighted.

## Law 2: Brand is Embedded, Not Applied
The Eli brand should be felt through:
- The tone of the copy.
- Semantic color meaning.
- Generous spacing and typographic contrast.
- Restraint.

It should *not* be felt through repeated logos, decorative watermarks, playful illustrations, or loud brand colors painted across the UI.

## Law 3: Green is Earned
**Sage Green (`#2D7A52`)** is not a generic accent color. It is strictly reserved for:
- grounded confirmation
- meaningful completion
- trusted progress
- calm positive resolution

If you use it everywhere, it means nothing when the student actually earns it.

## Law 4: Terracotta Invites, It Does Not Command
Primary action buttons use **Muted Terracotta (`#B35C44`)**.
- CTAs should feel like a calm invitation into motion, not a demand for performance.
- They should *never* feel like urgent alerts, command-center prompts, or pressure-heavy productivity buttons.
- The color and the copy work together to lower the barrier to entry.

## Law 5: No Dashboard Behaviors
Eli actively rejects standard productivity software patterns that induce anxiety or guilt.
- **No Streaks:** Do not track consecutive days.
- **No Progress Bars:** Do not use percentage bars (e.g., "40% complete") which trigger completion anxiety.
- **No Guilt Metrics:** Never show a counter of "days missed."
- **No Extrinsic Gamification:** No badges, leaderboards, or points. 

Progress should be expressed in grounded, non-performative ways, and identity should be reflected through earned meaning rather than reward mechanics.
