# Brand States

Eli's expression adapts to the student's current capacity. These are not fixed user types. They are operating conditions. A student can move between them over time, within a week, or even within a single day.

This document is only about **current-capacity expression**. It does not define relationship maturity. That belongs in the brand-phase model.

The brand uses **three operating states** and **one reflective layer**:
- `Shelter`
- `Focus`
- `Momentum`
- `Mirror` (reflective layer)

## 1. Shelter

`Shelter` is the state Eli uses when the student needs less pressure, less complexity, and more permission to begin.

- **Emotional job:** Reduce threat. Make the student feel safe enough to continue.
- **Visual temperature:** Warmest and softest version of the system.
- **Density:** Minimal. One clear center. Fewer competing decisions.
- **Copy posture:** Grounding, brief, non-interrogative, and emotionally steady.
- **What Eli reduces:** Choice count, visual contrast, urgency, explanatory language.
- **What to avoid:** Any tone or structure that makes the student feel judged, behind, or evaluated.

## 2. Focus

`Focus` is the default operating state when the student can hold a normal amount of structure and move through the product without extra emotional cushioning.

- **Emotional job:** Organize attention. Make the next step clear.
- **Visual temperature:** Balanced and neutral. Warm, but not especially softened.
- **Density:** Moderate. Enough structure to orient, but not enough to clutter.
- **Copy posture:** Practical, clear, and direct without becoming managerial.
- **What Eli reduces:** Ambiguity, scattered emphasis, unnecessary detours.
- **What to avoid:** Over-supporting or over-explaining ordinary progress.

## 3. Momentum

`Momentum` is the state Eli uses when the student can tolerate more decisiveness, more definition, and a slightly stronger sense of forward motion.

- **Emotional job:** Focus energy without turning it into pressure.
- **Visual temperature:** Cleaner and slightly crisper, while still recognizably Eli.
- **Density:** Moderate-to-rich. Enough structure to support challenge and direction.
- **Copy posture:** Respectful, more decisive, and still emotionally calm.
- **What Eli reduces:** Hesitation, diffuse attention, overly cautious framing.
- **What to avoid:** Competitive energy, hype, or challenge language that feels performative.

## Mirror Layer

`Mirror` is not a peer operating state. It is a reflective layer that can appear on top of `Focus` or `Momentum` when Eli has earned the right to say something more meaningful than a standard instruction or nudge.

- **Emotional job:** Turn observed behavior into meaning without sounding presumptuous.
- **Visual temperature:** Quiet and sparse. The reflection should carry the emphasis, not the chrome.
- **Density:** Minimal around the reflection so the student can actually receive it.
- **Copy posture:** Specific, grounded, and earned. Never aspirational for its own sake.
- **What Eli reduces:** Functional clutter, secondary emphasis, decorative motion.
- **What to avoid:** Premature intimacy, identity claims unsupported by evidence, or reflective language that sounds scripted.
