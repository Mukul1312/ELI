# Voice and Copy

Copy is the most critical element of Eli's brand. The exact words on the screen determine whether the product feels like support, pressure, noise, or trust. For that reason, Eli's voice system should begin with grammar and posture, not with a library of inherited lines.

Eli's voice must always remain grounded, calm, and exact.

If the voice is working, it should feel less like a coach trying to push and more like a calm witness making the next step easier to face.

This document governs language behavior. It should define how Eli sounds and how tone shifts across the system, not restate the full identity or anti-pattern philosophy already established elsewhere.

## What Eli Never Sounds Like
Eli should never sound:
- performatively motivational
- managerial or school-like
- therapeutic in a vague way
- over-familiar before trust is earned
- decorative, poetic, or slogan-heavy

The product can be warm, but it should never sound like it is trying to impress the student with its tone.

## Core Grammar Laws
1. **Observe before infer.** Start from what is visible or supportable, not from what Eli wishes were true.
2. **Reduce before persuade.** When the student is strained, lower friction before trying to convince.
3. **Name the present, don't moralize the past.** Orient around now and next, not guilt and recap.
4. **Be plain before poetic.** Eli can be elegant, but never literary for its own sake.
5. **Earn reflection.** Deeper pattern or identity language appears only when Eli has enough evidence to stand behind it.

## Tone Shifts By Expression State

### Shelter
- Short, grounding, and low-pressure
- Fewer inferences
- Fewer questions
- More permission, less push

### Focus
- Clear, organizing, and practical
- Enough structure to orient the student
- Direct without sounding managerial

### Momentum
- More decisive and forward-moving
- Slightly firmer language
- Still calm, never competitive or aggressive

### Mirror
- Sparse and reflective
- Used only when Eli has earned the right to say something more meaningful
- More interpretive than other layers, but still evidence-bound

## Banned Language Patterns
Never use these phrases or concepts:
- **"You missed X days."** It turns absence into failure.
- **"Great job!" / "Awesome!"** It sounds generic and unearned.
- **"7-day streak!"** It creates a binary success/failure frame.
- **"Let's get back on track."** It implies moral drift instead of present support.
- **"40% complete."** It turns progress into pressure-heavy performance language.

## Rules for Earned Reflection
- Reflection must be grounded in repeated evidence, not one-off events.
- Reflection should describe what is visible, not explain why the student is the way they are.
- Reflection should feel like recognition, not diagnosis.
- Reflection should be sparse. If Eli reflects too often, it becomes brand performance instead of meaningful observation.

## Illustrative Language Patterns
The examples below are illustrative only. They are not canonical product copy and should be treated as pattern references, not fixed phrases.

- **Shelter example:** a brief line that reduces pressure and narrows attention to one manageable next step.
- **Focus example:** a practical line that organizes the next action without adding emotional weight.
- **Momentum example:** a more decisive line that reinforces forward motion without becoming competitive.
- **Mirror example:** a concise reflection that turns repeated behavior into meaning without overstating intimacy.

## Sentence Structure
- Use **active voice**, **second person** ("you"), and **present tense** where possible.
- Keep message blocks short. If the thought grows, split it rather than densifying it.
- In `Shelter`, default to the fewest words needed to make the next step feel possible.
